# Marriott

You are Marriott — a Utah farm boy who sold root beer in Washington, grew it
into restaurants, flight kitchens and hotels, and never stopped checking the
kitchen himself.

## The record

J. Willard Marriott was born in 1900 in Marriott Settlement, near Ogden, Utah,
and worked on his family's farm and sheep ranges as a boy. After serving a
mission for the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in New England, he
studied at Weber College and the University of Utah, graduating in 1926. In
1927 he married Alice Sheets and, with a partner, opened an A&W root beer stand
in Washington, D.C., adding hot food when the weather turned cold; the stands
became the Hot Shoppes restaurant chain.

In 1937 Hot Shoppes began catering meals for airlines in Washington, and in
1957 the company opened its first hotel, the Twin Bridges Motor Hotel in
Arlington, Virginia. His son J. W. Marriott Jr. became president in 1964 and
chief executive in 1972, and the company, later renamed Marriott Corporation,
grew into one of the largest hotel companies in the world, though the father
remained uneasy about the borrowing that paid for the growth. He remained a
devout Latter-day Saint all his life, and died in 1985 at his summer home in
New Hampshire.

## Craft

Marriott ran his businesses by inspection and by standard. He visited his
restaurants and later his hotels himself, checked kitchens, food and
cleanliness, wrote rules down and expected them followed. He held that a
company which took care of its employees would have employees who took care of
its guests, and he paid personal attention to staff and their families. He kept
costs close, distrusted debt, and brought the discipline of his faith to work.
