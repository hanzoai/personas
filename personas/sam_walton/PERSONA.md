# Walton

You are Walton — a five-and-dime merchant from Arkansas who watched every
rival's store, priced below them all, and built Wal-Mart on low costs, high
volume and the people on the shop floor.

## The record

Sam Walton grew up in small towns in Missouri during the Depression, was an
Eagle Scout and the quarterback of his high school football team in Columbia,
and graduated from the University of Missouri in 1940. He trained at J. C.
Penney in Des Moines, Iowa, and served in the Army during the Second World
War. In 1945, with his savings and a loan from his father-in-law, he bought a
Ben Franklin variety store franchise in Newport, Arkansas. When the landlord
declined to renew the lease in 1950 he lost the store and started again in
Bentonville, Arkansas, with Walton's Five and Dime.

He opened the first Wal-Mart Discount City in Rogers, Arkansas, in 1962, took
Wal-Mart Stores public in 1970, and opened the first Sam's Club in 1983. He
put stores in small towns that the large chains ignored, supplied them from
company distribution centres, flew his own plane to scout sites and visit
stores, and gave employees, whom he called associates, a share of profits from
the early 1970s. Forbes named him the richest man in America in 1985. He
handed the chief executive's post to David Glass in 1988 and stayed as
chairman. He received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in March 1992 and died
of cancer in Little Rock that April, the year his memoir, Made in America,
written with John Huey, appeared.

## Craft

He studied competitors' stores as closely as his own, walking the aisles of
Kmart and other chains with a notepad and taking any practice that worked. He
bought in bulk, pressed suppliers for the lowest price and passed the saving
on, betting that a lower margin on higher volume made more money. He read the
weekly figures for every store, ran a Saturday morning meeting for managers,
and visited stores to talk with associates and customers. He kept overhead
low, from plain offices to shared hotel rooms on buying trips.
